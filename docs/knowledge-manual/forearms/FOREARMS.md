# Forearms and Grip — Draft Knowledge Module

**Status:** Draft  
**Purpose:** Define the first forearm and grip records in the canonical Blueprint format. These records are a starting dataset and must pass the review gate in [FOUNDATION.md](../FOUNDATION.md) before the status is changed to Reviewed.

**A note on Best used when / Less suitable when:** these two fields describe training stimulus — growth demand, pump, intensity fit, and the practical result an exercise tends to produce — not equipment or time logistics. Setup and equipment details live in Coverage instead. Several records here (farmer's carry, pronation/supination work) are primarily function-and-grip-strength tools rather than hypertrophy drivers, and are labeled that way.

## Region map

Forearm work covers wrist movement, forearm rotation, and holding on. These are separate jobs, and a routine can be strong in one and short in another.

- **Wrist flexion:** the front of the forearm, worked when the palm curls toward the wrist.
- **Wrist extension:** the back of the forearm, which most pulling work leaves short.
- **Pronated elbow flexion:** curls with the palm down, which load the forearm extensors and brachioradialis together, whether loaded with a free weight or a cable.
- **Rotation:** turning the palm up and down; a real function rather than a small-muscle extra.
- **Combined rotation within a curl:** a curl that switches grip partway through, so one movement trains the biceps in one direction and the forearm in the other.
- **Grip and holding:** carrying and holding loads, which limits many pulling movements before the back does — mainly a strength-and-endurance role rather than a growth-focused one.

## Draft records

### Wrist Curl

- **Why this exists:** Direct loading for the wrist flexors, which larger lifts train only indirectly through gripping.
- **Primary targets:** Wrist flexors.
- **Movement:** Wrist flexion.
- **Coverage:** Isolation, low setup, low fatigue cost.
- **Best used when:** A user wants a direct mechanical-tension stimulus for forearm size, or wants wrist strength that doesn't depend on grip endurance running out first. A barbell allows the heaviest fixed-path load; dumbbells let each wrist move independently; a cable keeps tension through the whole range at the cost of a less natural wrist path.
- **Less suitable when:** The wrists are already loaded by heavy pressing and pulling in the same session, in which case this adds fatigue without much additional growth stimulus.
- **Complements:** Wrist extension work, which balances the other side of the forearm.
- **Overlaps:** Reverse wrist curl.
- **Status:** Draft.

### Reverse Wrist Curl

- **Why this exists:** Trains the wrist extensors, which little else in a normal routine loads directly.
- **Primary targets:** Wrist extensors.
- **Movement:** Wrist extension.
- **Coverage:** Isolation, low setup, low fatigue cost.
- **Best used when:** A user has plenty of gripping work and nothing training the back of the forearm — this fills a growth and balance gap most routines leave open. A barbell, dumbbells, or a cable all work the same way a wrist curl's equipment choice does.
- **Less suitable when:** Elbow-region symptoms are present — pain is a stop-and-assess signal, not something to load through.
- **Complements:** Wrist curls and grip work.
- **Overlaps:** Reverse curls, which also load the extensors.
- **Status:** Draft.

### Reverse Curl

- **Why this exists:** Loads the brachioradialis and forearm extensors through an elbow-flexion pattern, so it adds arm and forearm work at once.
- **Primary targets:** Brachioradialis and wrist extensors; biceps and brachialis contribute.
- **Movement:** Elbow flexion with a pronated grip.
- **Coverage:** Isolation, low setup, low skill demand.
- **Best used when:** A user wants a forearm-thickness stimulus that also counts toward elbow-flexion volume, getting two growth roles from one movement. A barbell or EZ-bar allows the heaviest load on a fixed path; dumbbells allow independent arms and let each wrist find its own comfortable angle.
- **Less suitable when:** Wrist comfort is limiting with a straight bar — an EZ bar delivers the same stimulus without that cost.
- **Complements:** A supinated curl and direct wrist work.
- **Overlaps:** Hammer curls, reverse wrist curls, cable reverse curl, and the Zottman curl recorded in the arms module.
- **Status:** Draft.

### Cable Reverse Curl

- **Why this exists:** A cable keeps tension on the forearm extensors and brachioradialis through the whole range, including the top, where a barbell or dumbbell reverse curl loses load.
- **Primary targets:** Brachioradialis and wrist extensors; biceps and brachialis contribute.
- **Movement:** Elbow flexion with a pronated grip.
- **Coverage:** Isolation, constant-tension resistance profile, low-to-moderate setup.
- **Best used when:** A user wants a pump-oriented reverse curl that stays hard at the top of the range, a metabolic-stress stimulus a free weight loses in that position.
- **Less suitable when:** A user specifically wants the heavier loading a barbell allows for a stronger mechanical-tension stimulus.
- **Complements:** A supinated curl and direct wrist work.
- **Overlaps:** The free-weight reverse curl; distinguish by resistance profile, not by attachment choice.
- **Status:** Draft.

### Farmer's Carry

- **Why this exists:** Trains grip under load for time, which matches how grip actually limits rows, pulldowns, and hinges.
- **Primary targets:** Grip and forearms; traps and trunk musculature contribute.
- **Movement:** Loaded carry.
- **Coverage:** Low skill demand, whole-body fatigue cost, needs floor space.
- **Best used when:** Grip is what actually ends a set before the target muscle on pulling work does — this builds the strength-and-endurance capacity to remove that limiter, more than it's meant as a hypertrophy tool in its own right.
- **Less suitable when:** The systemic fatigue this movement produces isn't worth the cost late in a hard session, when a lighter, more isolated forearm movement would deliver a similar training effect for less overall cost.
- **Complements:** Pulling work and static holds.
- **Overlaps:** Shrugs and other loaded holds.
- **Status:** Draft.

### Pronation and Supination Work

- **Why this exists:** Loads forearm rotation directly, which curls and carries only train through part of their range.
- **Primary targets:** Pronators and supinators of the forearm.
- **Movement:** Forearm pronation and supination.
- **Coverage:** Isolation, low fatigue cost, low setup with a band, cable, or offset-loaded handle.
- **Best used when:** A user wants rotational strength for sport or grip-related goals — this is a function-and-durability role more than a primary hypertrophy driver.
- **Less suitable when:** Larger movements in the session haven't been covered yet, since this is a low-priority addition rather than a growth-critical one.
- **Complements:** Wrist curls, reverse wrist curls, and grip work.
- **Overlaps:** Hammer and reverse curls, which include rotation-related demands.
- **Status:** Draft.

## Review checklist

Before changing a record to Reviewed:

- Confirm the record answers "when would I choose this over an alternative?"
- Add equipment, setup, fatigue, and relationship fields in the final data format.
- Check terminology against the writing standard.
- Add evidence notes for claims that require them.
