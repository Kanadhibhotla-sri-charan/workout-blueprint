# Physique Blueprint — Muscle Map

**Status:** Draft knowledge map  
**Writing style:** Simple first. The goal is to help a person understand what to train and why, without needing an anatomy degree.

## How to use this map

A muscle name tells you where the work is happening. The exercise choice tells you what job it does in a routine. Most people do not need an exercise for every line below; this map stops important areas from being forgotten.

## Chest

- **Upper chest:** The area closer to the collarbone. Incline pressing and low-to-high cable work are common ways to train it.
- **Mid chest:** The central chest area. Flat presses are common choices.
- **Lower chest:** The lower part of the chest. Dips, decline-style presses, and some cable paths may fit here.

## Back

- **Lats:** Help create the wider look from the front and back. Vertical pulls and some rowing patterns train them.
- **Mid-back and rhomboids:** Help you pull the shoulder blades back. Rows are the main family here.
- **Traps:** Upper traps sit near the neck; mid and lower traps help control the shoulder blades.
- **Spinal erectors:** Support the spine during hinges and many standing lifts.

## Shoulders and shoulder health

- **Front delts:** Help with pressing. They already get plenty of work from many chest presses.
- **Side delts:** Build shoulder width. Lateral raise variations are the direct choice.
- **Rear delts:** Help balance the shoulder and add shape from the side and back. Rear-delt fly and row variations help.
- **Rotator cuff:** Small muscles that help keep the shoulder joint controlled. Light rotation and shoulder-control work can be useful, but pain needs professional assessment.
- **Serratus anterior:** Helps the shoulder blade move well around the rib cage. Push-up-plus and wall-slide style work can train it.

## Arms and forearms

- **Biceps:** Bend the elbow and help turn the palm up.
- **Brachialis:** Sits under the biceps and can add arm thickness. Neutral-grip curls are a common option.
- **Brachioradialis:** Runs into the forearm and works hard during hammer-style curls.
- **Triceps:** Straighten the elbow and make up a large share of upper-arm size.
- **Forearm flexors and extensors:** Control the wrist and support gripping.
- **Pronation and supination:** Turn the palm down and up. These are useful functions, not just “small-muscle extras.”

## Core

- **Rectus abdominis:** The visible abdominal wall. It helps bend and control the trunk.
- **Obliques:** Help turn and resist turning. They also help the torso stay steady.
- **Transverse abdominis:** Deep abdominal support that helps brace the trunk.
- **Quadratus lumborum:** Helps control side bending and pelvic position.

## Hip complex and glutes

- **Gluteus maximus:** Main hip-extension muscle. Hip thrusts, bridges, squats, and hinges can train it.
- **Gluteus medius and minimus:** Help keep the pelvis steady and move the leg out to the side.
- **Hip flexors:** Lift the thigh. They matter for movement and can be trained, not only stretched.
- **Adductors:** Bring the leg toward the midline and help in squatting patterns.
- **Abductors and TFL:** Help move and stabilize the leg to the side.

## Legs

- **Quads:** Straighten the knee. Squats, leg presses, and leg extensions are common choices.
- **Hamstrings:** Bend the knee and help extend the hip. A good plan usually includes both a hinge pattern and a leg-curl pattern.
- **Calves — gastrocnemius (inner and outer heads):** Crosses the knee and ankle and gives the calf its visible shape; standing calf raises are a common option.
- **Calves — soleus:** Works strongly when the knee is bent and adds thickness lower on the leg; seated calf raises are a common option.
- **Tibialis anterior:** Lifts the foot upward. Tibialis raises are a direct option.

## Neck

- **Neck flexors, extensors, and side flexors:** Help control head position. Train cautiously and avoid loading through pain or symptoms.

Some movements appear in more than one module — the Romanian deadlift in hips and hamstrings, the Bulgarian split squat in hips and quads, the 45° back extension in back and hamstrings. That is recorded deliberately, and those records must be reconciled before any of them is marked Reviewed.

Some movements split into two records because torso or joint position changes which muscle takes the emphasis — the dip appears in chest (forward lean) and arms (upright torso), and the conventional deadlift (back) sits alongside the Romanian deadlift (hips, hamstrings) as a related but distinct hip hinge. These are separate variations under the separate-variation rule in the PDD, not duplicates.
