# All Muscle Modules — Draft

Every section uses the same rule: choose exercises because they do a different job, not because they have a different name.

## Back

### Lats
- **Pull-up or pulldown:** Use this when your main goal is a vertical pulling movement. Keep in mind that grip, equipment, and strength level change the best option.
- **Straight-arm pulldown:** Use this when you want a simpler lat-focused movement without another hard elbow-bending exercise.
- **Row with elbows close:** Use this when you want rowing work that also keeps the lats involved.

### Mid-back
- **Chest-supported row:** Use this when you want to row hard without your lower back becoming the limiting factor.
- **Cable row:** Use this when you want an easy-to-control row with a simple setup.
- **Barbell row:** Use this when you want a demanding free-weight row and can keep a stable hip position.

## Shoulders

### Side delts
- **Dumbbell lateral raise:** The basic shoulder-width exercise. Use it when you can control the weight without swinging.
- **Cable lateral raise:** Use it when you want steady tension and easy small adjustments.
- **Machine lateral raise:** Use it when you want a stable option and do not want balance to limit the set.

### Rear delts
- **Reverse fly:** Use it when you want direct rear-shoulder work.
- **Face pull:** Use it when you want rear-shoulder and upper-back work together; do not turn it into a heavy ego lift.
- **Rear-delt row:** Use it when you prefer a rowing movement but want the elbows to travel out rather than close to the body.

## Arms

### Biceps and brachialis
- **Dumbbell curl:** A simple biceps option. Use it when you want each arm to work independently.
- **Incline curl:** Use it when you are comfortable controlling the shoulder position and want a stricter curl.
- **Hammer curl:** Use it when you want biceps, brachialis, and forearm involvement together.
- **Preacher curl:** Use it when you want to reduce body swing and make the curl easier to repeat.

### Triceps
- **Cable pushdown:** Use it for a simple, low-setup triceps exercise.
- **Overhead cable extension:** Use it when you want an overhead triceps option and it feels comfortable.
- **Close-grip press:** Use it when you want a heavier press that also trains the triceps.
- **Single-arm cable extension:** Use it when you need an easy way to match each arm.

## Forearms and grip

- **Farmer carry:** Use it when you want simple grip work that also trains posture and bracing.
- **Wrist curl and extension:** Use these when you want direct forearm work; keep loading controlled.
- **Pronation and supination work:** Use these for wrist-turning strength, with light and careful loading.

## Core

- **Cable crunch:** Use it when you want a loadable abdominal curl.
- **Hanging knee raise:** Use it when you can control the pelvis instead of only swinging the legs.
- **Dead bug:** Use it when you want simple trunk control and bracing practice.
- **Pallof press:** Use it when you want to resist twisting rather than create it.
- **Side plank:** Use it when you want simple side-of-torso control without equipment.

## Hip complex and glutes

- **Hip thrust:** Use it when you want a direct hip-extension exercise with a supported upper body.
- **Romanian deadlift:** Use it when you want a hip hinge that trains glutes and hamstrings together.
- **Split squat:** Use it when you want each leg to work separately and can control balance.
- **Hip abduction machine or cable:** Use it when you want direct side-hip work.
- **Adductor machine:** Use it when you want direct inner-thigh work.

## Quads

- **Squat:** Use it when you want a big knee-and-hip movement and can use a comfortable depth and setup.
- **Leg press:** Use it when you want hard leg work with less balance demand.
- **Leg extension:** Use it when you want direct knee-extension work without another full-body lift.
- **Split squat:** Use it when you want one-leg work and a different challenge from bilateral squats.

## Hamstrings

- **Romanian deadlift:** Use it for the hip-hinge role.
- **Seated leg curl:** Use it for direct knee-bending work.
- **Lying leg curl:** Another knee-bending option; choose the version that feels and fits better.
- **Nordic curl progression:** Use it only when you can control the lowering phase and progress gradually.

A complete hamstring plan often uses both a hinge and a curl.

## Calves and lower leg

- **Standing calf raise:** Use it when you want a straight-leg calf option.
- **Seated calf raise:** Use it when you want a bent-knee calf option.
- **Tibialis raise:** Use it when you want direct work for lifting the foot upward.

## Neck

- **Light neck flexion, extension, and side-flexion:** Use only with gradual loading and good control. Stop and seek qualified help for pain, tingling, dizziness, or other symptoms.

## Review rule

Each record above is a draft. Before it becomes Reviewed, add its equipment options, setup time, fatigue level, technique cues, alternatives, overlap, and evidence notes.
