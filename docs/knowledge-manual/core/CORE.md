# Core — Draft Knowledge Module

**Status:** Draft  
**Purpose:** Define the first core records in the canonical Blueprint format. These records are a starting dataset and must pass the review gate in [FOUNDATION.md](../FOUNDATION.md) before the status is changed to Reviewed.

**A note on Best used when / Less suitable when:** these two fields describe training stimulus — growth demand, pump, intensity fit, and the practical result an exercise tends to produce — not equipment or time logistics. Setup and equipment details live in Coverage instead. Several records here (plank, Pallof press, suitcase carry) are primarily stability-and-control tools rather than hypertrophy drivers, and are labeled that way. Visible abdominal definition depends largely on overall body composition, not on any single exercise here.

## Region map

Core work splits into moving the trunk and resisting it being moved. Most routines cover one and forget the other.

- **Trunk flexion:** bending the trunk against resistance; the loadable, mechanical-tension abdominal family.
- **Hip flexion with trunk control:** raising the legs while keeping the pelvis and ribs under control.
- **Anti-extension:** resisting the lower back arching under load — mostly a control-and-stability role.
- **Anti-rotation:** resisting the trunk being twisted — mostly a control-and-stability role.
- **Anti-lateral flexion:** resisting the trunk being pulled sideways — mostly a control-and-stability role.
- **Rotation:** rotating the trunk against resistance, a mechanical-tension stimulus rather than resisting rotation.

## Draft records

### Cable Crunch

- **Why this exists:** A trunk-flexion movement with adjustable load, so abdominal work can progress the way other lifts do.
- **Primary targets:** Rectus abdominis; obliques contribute.
- **Movement:** Trunk flexion.
- **Coverage:** Isolation, small load increments, moderate setup.
- **Best used when:** A user wants a genuine mechanical-tension growth stimulus for the abs, progressing load over time rather than adding reps indefinitely.
- **Less suitable when:** The hips take over the movement, which shifts the stimulus away from the abs and toward hip flexion instead.
- **Complements:** An anti-rotation or anti-extension movement.
- **Overlaps:** Machine crunch; distinguish by resistance profile.
- **Status:** Draft.

### Machine Crunch

- **Why this exists:** A fixed-path trunk-flexion movement that removes the cable-height setup of a cable crunch, often with a cam that adjusts resistance through the range.
- **Primary targets:** Rectus abdominis; obliques contribute.
- **Movement:** Trunk flexion.
- **Coverage:** Isolation, low skill demand, low fatigue cost.
- **Best used when:** A user wants a mechanical-tension ab stimulus pushed safely to true failure, since the fixed path removes any technique variability that would otherwise limit the load.
- **Less suitable when:** The machine's pad position doesn't suit the individual, which can blunt the tension felt regardless of how heavy the stack is loaded.
- **Complements:** An anti-rotation or anti-extension movement.
- **Overlaps:** Cable crunch; distinguish by resistance profile.
- **Status:** Draft.

### Hanging Knee or Leg Raise

- **Why this exists:** Trains hip flexion with trunk control, and scales from bent knees to straight legs without changing equipment.
- **Primary targets:** Hip flexors and rectus abdominis; obliques and grip contribute.
- **Movement:** Hip flexion with trunk flexion.
- **Coverage:** Low equipment, moderate-to-high skill demand, scalable.
- **Best used when:** A user wants a hip-flexor-and-ab growth stimulus that scales purely through range and leg position, without needing added load.
- **Less suitable when:** Grip fails first or the movement turns into swinging, both of which drain the set of the controlled tension that gives it its growth value.
- **Complements:** A loaded trunk-flexion movement.
- **Overlaps:** Captain's chair and lying leg raise variations.
- **Status:** Draft.

### Plank

- **Why this exists:** The lowest-barrier anti-extension option, with no equipment and almost no skill requirement to start.
- **Primary targets:** Rectus abdominis and transverse abdominis; glutes and shoulders contribute.
- **Movement:** Anti-extension isometric.
- **Coverage:** Low equipment, low setup, low fatigue cost.
- **Best used when:** A user wants trunk-bracing control and endurance rather than a growth stimulus — this is a stability tool first, and a beginner-friendly entry point into anti-extension work.
- **Less suitable when:** Hold times have grown long enough that the set is mostly endurance — at that point the stimulus has plateaued, and a harder variation or added load delivers more actual growth demand for the same time spent.
- **Complements:** A loaded flexion movement and an anti-rotation movement.
- **Overlaps:** Ab wheel rollouts and other anti-extension work.
- **Status:** Draft.

### Ab Wheel Rollout

- **Why this exists:** An anti-extension movement that moves through a range instead of holding a position, and scales by how far the user rolls out.
- **Primary targets:** Rectus abdominis; lats, hip flexors, and shoulders contribute.
- **Movement:** Anti-extension through range.
- **Coverage:** Low equipment, high skill demand, moderate fatigue cost.
- **Best used when:** A user wants a real mechanical-tension and eccentric-control stimulus once planks have stopped delivering one, since moving through a lengthening range under control is a much harder demand than holding a static position.
- **Less suitable when:** The lower back arches during the rollout, or the range can't yet be controlled — both signal the load has outrun what the trunk can actually brace against.
- **Complements:** An anti-rotation movement.
- **Overlaps:** Planks and long-lever anti-extension variations.
- **Status:** Draft.

### Pallof Press

- **Why this exists:** Loads anti-rotation directly, a role that crunches and planks do not cover.
- **Primary targets:** Obliques; rectus abdominis and hip musculature contribute.
- **Movement:** Anti-rotation isometric.
- **Coverage:** Isolation-oriented, low fatigue cost, low setup with a band.
- **Best used when:** A user wants trunk control and stability that transfers to standing, carrying, and single-side loading — this is a control-and-durability role more than a primary hypertrophy driver.
- **Less suitable when:** The hips rotate along with the load, which defeats the anti-rotation demand that's the entire point of the movement.
- **Complements:** Trunk flexion and anti-extension work.
- **Overlaps:** Anti-rotation holds and cable chops.
- **Status:** Draft.

### Suitcase Carry

- **Why this exists:** Loads one side at a time so the trunk resists being pulled sideways, while also training grip.
- **Primary targets:** Obliques and quadratus lumborum; grip and traps contribute.
- **Movement:** Anti-lateral flexion under load.
- **Coverage:** Low skill demand, whole-body fatigue cost, needs floor space.
- **Best used when:** A user wants standing trunk-control work that carries over to real-world single-side loading — a stability-and-durability role more than a primary hypertrophy driver.
- **Less suitable when:** Grip fails long before the trunk does, which ends the set before the intended stimulus is really reached.
- **Complements:** Anti-rotation and trunk-flexion work.
- **Overlaps:** Side planks and farmer's carries.
- **Status:** Draft.

### Cable Woodchop

- **Why this exists:** Trains trunk rotation through a range, the opposite job from the anti-rotation work above.
- **Primary targets:** Obliques; rectus abdominis and hip musculature contribute.
- **Movement:** Trunk rotation.
- **Coverage:** Isolation-oriented, low fatigue cost, moderate setup with a cable.
- **Best used when:** A user wants a pump-oriented, constant-tension rotational stimulus for the obliques, alongside anti-rotation work rather than instead of it.
- **Less suitable when:** The movement is driven by the arms rather than the trunk, which shifts the stimulus away from the obliques entirely.
- **Complements:** The Pallof press and other anti-rotation work.
- **Overlaps:** Russian twist; distinguish by resistance profile.
- **Status:** Draft.

### Russian Twist

- **Why this exists:** A free-weight rotation option; the load is hardest at the ends of the range and eases through the middle, the opposite feel from a cable woodchop's constant tension.
- **Primary targets:** Obliques; rectus abdominis and hip musculature contribute.
- **Movement:** Trunk rotation.
- **Coverage:** Isolation-oriented, low fatigue cost, low setup with a dumbbell, plate, or medicine ball.
- **Best used when:** A user wants a rotational stimulus loaded like a free weight, hardest at the ends of the range rather than held constant like a cable.
- **Less suitable when:** The movement turns into fast, uncontrolled swinging rather than a controlled rotation, which trades the growth stimulus for momentum.
- **Complements:** The Pallof press and other anti-rotation work.
- **Overlaps:** Cable woodchop; distinguish by resistance profile.
- **Status:** Draft.

## Review checklist

Before changing a record to Reviewed:

- Confirm the record answers "when would I choose this over an alternative?"
- Add equipment, setup, fatigue, and relationship fields in the final data format.
- Check terminology against the writing standard.
- Add evidence notes for claims that require them.
