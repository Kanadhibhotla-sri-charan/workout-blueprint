# Core — Draft Knowledge Module

**Status:** Draft  
**Purpose:** Define the first core records in the canonical Blueprint format. These records are a starting dataset and must pass the review gate in [FOUNDATION.md](../FOUNDATION.md) before the status is changed to Reviewed.

## Region map

Core work splits into moving the trunk and resisting it being moved. Most routines cover one and forget the other. Visible abdominal definition depends largely on overall body composition, so these records describe training roles rather than a promised appearance.

- **Trunk flexion:** bending the trunk against resistance; the loadable abdominal family.
- **Hip flexion with trunk control:** raising the legs while keeping the pelvis and ribs under control.
- **Anti-extension:** resisting the lower back arching under load.
- **Anti-rotation:** resisting the trunk being twisted.
- **Anti-lateral flexion:** resisting the trunk being pulled sideways.

## Draft records

### Cable Crunch

- **Why this exists:** A trunk-flexion movement with adjustable load, so abdominal work can progress the way other lifts do.
- **Primary targets:** Rectus abdominis; obliques contribute.
- **Movement:** Trunk flexion.
- **Coverage:** Isolation, small load increments, moderate setup.
- **Best used when:** A user wants progressive abdominal work rather than adding repetitions indefinitely.
- **Less suitable when:** Cable access is limited, or the hips take over the movement.
- **Complements:** An anti-rotation or anti-extension movement.
- **Overlaps:** Machine crunches and weighted sit-up variations.
- **Status:** Draft.

### Hanging Knee or Leg Raise

- **Why this exists:** Trains hip flexion with trunk control, and scales from bent knees to straight legs without changing equipment.
- **Primary targets:** Hip flexors and rectus abdominis; obliques and grip contribute.
- **Movement:** Hip flexion with trunk flexion.
- **Coverage:** Low equipment, moderate-to-high skill demand, scalable.
- **Best used when:** A bar is available and a user wants abdominal work that scales without added load.
- **Less suitable when:** Grip fails first, or the movement turns into swinging.
- **Complements:** A loaded trunk-flexion movement.
- **Overlaps:** Captain's chair and lying leg raise variations.
- **Status:** Draft.

### Plank

- **Why this exists:** The lowest-barrier anti-extension option, with no equipment and almost no skill requirement to start.
- **Primary targets:** Rectus abdominis and transverse abdominis; glutes and shoulders contribute.
- **Movement:** Anti-extension isometric.
- **Coverage:** Low equipment, low setup, low fatigue cost.
- **Best used when:** A user is starting out, training at home, or wants trunk work that is easy to recover from.
- **Less suitable when:** Hold times have grown long enough that the set is mostly endurance — a harder variation or added load is usually the better step.
- **Complements:** A loaded flexion movement and an anti-rotation movement.
- **Overlaps:** Ab wheel rollouts and other anti-extension work.
- **Status:** Draft.

### Ab Wheel Rollout

- **Why this exists:** An anti-extension movement that moves through a range instead of holding a position, and scales by how far the user rolls out.
- **Primary targets:** Rectus abdominis; lats, hip flexors, and shoulders contribute.
- **Movement:** Anti-extension through range.
- **Coverage:** Low equipment, high skill demand, moderate fatigue cost.
- **Best used when:** Planks have stopped being challenging and a user wants a harder anti-extension option.
- **Less suitable when:** The lower back arches during the rollout, or the user cannot yet control a partial range.
- **Complements:** An anti-rotation movement.
- **Overlaps:** Planks and long-lever anti-extension variations.
- **Status:** Draft.

### Pallof Press

- **Why this exists:** Loads anti-rotation directly, a role that crunches and planks do not cover.
- **Primary targets:** Obliques; rectus abdominis and hip musculature contribute.
- **Movement:** Anti-rotation isometric.
- **Coverage:** Isolation-oriented, low fatigue cost, low setup with a band.
- **Best used when:** A user wants trunk work that transfers to standing, carrying, and single-side loading.
- **Less suitable when:** No cable or band is available, or the hips rotate with the load.
- **Complements:** Trunk flexion and anti-extension work.
- **Overlaps:** Anti-rotation holds and cable chops.
- **Status:** Draft.

### Suitcase Carry

- **Why this exists:** Loads one side at a time so the trunk resists being pulled sideways, while also training grip.
- **Primary targets:** Obliques and quadratus lumborum; grip and traps contribute.
- **Movement:** Anti-lateral flexion under load.
- **Coverage:** Low skill demand, whole-body fatigue cost, needs floor space.
- **Best used when:** A user wants standing trunk work, or wants grip and trunk covered together.
- **Less suitable when:** Space is limited, or grip fails long before the trunk does.
- **Complements:** Anti-rotation and trunk-flexion work.
- **Overlaps:** Side planks and farmer's carries.
- **Status:** Draft.

## Review checklist

Before changing a record to Reviewed:

- Confirm the record answers "when would I choose this over an alternative?"
- Add equipment, setup, fatigue, and relationship fields in the final data format.
- Check terminology against the writing standard.
- Add evidence notes for claims that require them.
