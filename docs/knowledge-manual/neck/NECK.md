# Neck — Draft Knowledge Module

**Status:** Draft  
**Purpose:** Define the first neck records in the canonical Blueprint format. These records are a starting dataset and must pass the review gate in [FOUNDATION.md](../FOUNDATION.md) before the status is changed to Reviewed.

**A note on Best used when / Less suitable when:** these two fields describe training stimulus — growth demand, intensity fit, and the practical result an exercise tends to produce — not equipment or time logistics. For this module specifically, safety and load-tolerance considerations take priority over growth framing wherever the two conflict.

## Region map

Neck training is optional for most goals and is usually chosen for appearance, for sport, or for general head-and-neck control. It is also the region where conservative loading matters most.

- **Extension:** moving the head backward against resistance — commonly the region most associated with visible neck thickness.
- **Flexion:** moving the head forward against resistance.
- **Lateral flexion:** moving the head toward the shoulder against resistance.
- **Isometric control:** resisting movement without the head travelling, which is the lowest-barrier way to start and the safest way to build tolerance before adding load.

Across every record in this module: load lightly, add load slowly, keep movement controlled, and stop at symptoms. Neck pain, headaches, dizziness, or any sensation travelling into the arms needs professional assessment rather than continued training.

## Draft records

### Isometric Neck Hold

- **Why this exists:** The lowest-barrier entry point to neck training, with no equipment and no head movement under load.
- **Primary targets:** Neck flexors, extensors, and lateral flexors, depending on the direction resisted.
- **Movement:** Neck isometric.
- **Coverage:** Low equipment, low setup, low skill demand.
- **Best used when:** A user is building initial tolerance and control before loaded work — this delivers a modest strength stimulus but is not a primary size driver on its own.
- **Less suitable when:** A user already tolerates loaded work well and wants a clearer path to progressive overload, which isometric holds are poorly suited to track over time.
- **Complements:** Loaded neck work introduced gradually afterward.
- **Overlaps:** Banded and manual-resistance neck holds.
- **Status:** Draft.

### Neck Extension

- **Why this exists:** Loads the back of the neck, which is what most neck-size goals are actually about, and which sitting posture rarely trains.
- **Primary targets:** Neck extensors; upper trapezius contributes.
- **Movement:** Neck extension.
- **Coverage:** Isolation, low fatigue cost, moderate setup with a harness or pad.
- **Best used when:** A user wants a genuine mechanical-tension growth stimulus for the area most responsible for visible neck thickness, and has already built control through isometric work.
- **Less suitable when:** Load or range can't yet be controlled — a lighter load with a slower tempo is the better next step, not a heavier one.
- **Complements:** Neck flexion work, so the region is not trained in one direction only.
- **Overlaps:** Harness, plate, and machine extension variations.
- **Status:** Draft.

### Neck Flexion

- **Why this exists:** Loads the front of the neck, which shrugs, rows, and extension work do not cover.
- **Primary targets:** Neck flexors.
- **Movement:** Neck flexion.
- **Coverage:** Isolation, low fatigue cost, low setup with a plate or band.
- **Best used when:** A user already trains neck extension and wants a balanced growth stimulus on both sides of the neck rather than one direction only.
- **Less suitable when:** Range or load can't be controlled, or symptoms appear at any point in the range — this is a stop signal, not something to push through for more stimulus.
- **Complements:** Neck extension work.
- **Overlaps:** Machine and manual-resistance flexion variations.
- **Status:** Draft.

### Lateral Neck Flexion

- **Why this exists:** Covers side-to-side neck strength, which straight flexion and extension leave out.
- **Primary targets:** Lateral neck flexors.
- **Movement:** Neck lateral flexion.
- **Coverage:** Isolation, unilateral, low fatigue cost, low setup.
- **Best used when:** A user wants complete, symmetric neck development rather than only the front-to-back directions, often for a sport-specific reason.
- **Less suitable when:** The basic front-to-back directions aren't yet trained comfortably, or symptoms are present — those come first.
- **Complements:** Flexion and extension work.
- **Overlaps:** Isometric side holds.
- **Status:** Draft.

## Review checklist

Before changing a record to Reviewed:

- Confirm the record answers "when would I choose this over an alternative?"
- Add equipment, setup, fatigue, and relationship fields in the final data format.
- Check terminology against the writing standard, especially the boundary between training guidance and medical advice.
- Add evidence notes for claims that require them.
