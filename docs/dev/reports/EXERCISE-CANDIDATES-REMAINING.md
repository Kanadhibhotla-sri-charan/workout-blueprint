## How each exercise is judged

The app recommends exercises through **muscle targets** (e.g. "side delts") and **functional goals** (e.g. "core anti-extension"). A new exercise improves the app only if it does at least one of these:

1. **Gives a thin muscle more options.** Several muscles have only 1–3 exercises to choose from.
2. **Opens a muscle to people with less equipment.** Home, bodyweight-only or dumbbell-only users.
3. **Does something no current exercise does.** A different movement, resistance profile or kind of user it suits.

An exercise that is only a slight variation of one already in the library usually belongs as a **note or extra equipment option on that exercise**, not as a new record. Otherwise it makes recommendations noisier rather than better.

## Verdicts

- **Strong:** clear new value; test and add first.
- **Worth testing:** some real value; measure before deciding.
- **Fold into existing:** a small variation; add it as an equipment option or coaching note on the existing exercise.
- **Low priority:** mostly a near-duplicate of something already there.

These verdicts are judgement, not measurement. Before anything is added it gets the same measured check as previous additions: how many empty Decide answers it fills, and which existing answers it changes.

## Strong

### 1. Upright row (wide grip, cable, EZ-bar or dumbbells)
- **What it is:** pull a bar or handles straight up the front of your body to about chest height, elbows leading out to the sides.
- **What it adds:**
  - The only pulling exercise that trains **side delts and upper traps together**, the two thinnest shoulder-area targets (3 and 2 options).
  - It also works with dumbbells alone, so it helps home users.
- **Watch out:** a narrow grip and pulling too high can irritate the shoulders. The record would specify a wide grip and stopping at chest height.
- **Equipment:** cable, EZ-bar, barbell or dumbbells. All already exist in the app.

### 2. Dead bug
- **What it is:** lie on your back with arms and legs up and slowly lower the opposite arm and leg while keeping your lower back flat on the floor.
- **What it adds:**
  - Only **one** exercise (the plank) answers the "core anti-extension" goal today.
  - The dead bug is the easiest, most back-friendly way to train it: floor only, very low skill.
  - It gives beginners and people with back sensitivity a real choice.
- **Equipment:** none.

### 3. Wrist roller
- **What it is:** a weight hangs on a rope from a short bar; roll the rope up and down by turning the bar with your wrists.
- **What it adds:**
  - Forearm flexors have **only one** exercise (the wrist curl), and extensors have three.
  - The wrist roller trains both directly, depending on roll direction, and gives a heavy, continuous forearm pump that the curls don't.
- **Watch out:** it needs a new equipment option, "wrist roller". A homemade one (stick, rope, plate) is easy and could be noted.
- **Equipment:** wrist roller (new) plus a plate.

### 4. Single-leg hip thrust
- **What it is:** a hip thrust with your upper back on a bench, done on one leg.
- **What it adds:**
  - The glute bridge is the only bodyweight glute-max option, and it becomes too easy quickly.
  - Doing it on one leg makes a home glute exercise hard enough for trained people without any weights.
- **Equipment:** bench (or sofa), bodyweight. Already exists in the app.

## Worth testing

### 5. Reverse crunch
- **What it is:** lie on your back and curl your knees toward your chest, lifting your hips off the floor.
- **What it adds:** an abs exercise that rolls the pelvis, like the hanging knee raise, but on the floor. The hanging knee raise needs a pull-up bar.
- **Question to measure:** the plank already gives abs a floor option, so it may change answers more than it fills gaps.

### 6. Sissy squat
- **What it is:** lean back while bending your knees forward, with your heels up, and lower your body.
- **What it adds:**
  - A bodyweight exercise that trains the quads in a deep stretch.
  - The only bodyweight quad option today is the reverse Nordic curl.
- **Watch out:** it is hard on the knees for some people and has a real learning curve.
- **Related bigger fix:** lunges and split squats currently need weights in the app. Adding a "bodyweight" option to them may help home quad training more than any new exercise.

### 7. Cable pull-through
- **What it is:** face away from a low cable, hinge at the hips, and pull the rope through your legs by standing up and squeezing your glutes.
- **What it adds:** a hip-hinge glute exercise with almost no lower-back load and very little to learn. It's a low-fatigue alternative to the RDL.
- **Question to measure:** the gym already has many glute options, so the real gain may be small.

### 8. Landmine press
- **What it is:** press one end of a barbell, anchored in a corner, up and forward at an angle.
- **What it adds:** a pressing option for people whose shoulders don't tolerate pressing straight overhead.
- **Watch out:**
  - The app has no "shoulder-friendly" filter, so it can't recommend it for that reason.
  - The value depends on whether the record's stability and skill ratings make it stand out.
- **Equipment:** landmine, barbell. Both already exist in the app.

### 9. Belt squat
- **What it is:** a squat where the weight hangs from a belt around your hips instead of sitting on your back.
- **What it adds:** heavy leg training with **no load on the spine**, for people with back issues.
- **Watch out:** the machine is uncommon in gyms, and quads already have 16 options.

### 10. Low-to-high cable fly as its own record
- **What it is:** a cable fly that sweeps the hands up from hip height to shoulder height.
- **What it adds:**
  - Today one "cable fly" record is tagged for upper, mid **and** lower chest at once.
  - Splitting out the low-to-high version would make upper-chest recommendations more precise.
- **Watch out:** this is really a data-precision change to the existing cable fly, not a new exercise. It would change the cable fly's own tags too.

## Fold into existing

Each of these is a real variation, but it doesn't do something new enough to deserve its own record. The cleaner change is an extra equipment option or a coaching note on the existing exercise.

| # | Exercise | Fold into | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | Barbell glute bridge | `glute-bridge` | Add a barbell option. The loaded version is the same movement as the bodyweight one, with a bar across the hips |
| 12 | Lean-away cable lateral raise | `cable-lateral-raise` | A coaching note: leaning away keeps more tension at the bottom of the movement |
| 13 | Heel-elevated goblet squat | `goblet-squat` | A coaching note: raising the heels lets the knees travel further forward, so the quads work harder |
| 14 | Smith machine / donkey calf raise | `standing-calf-raise` | Add Smith machine as an equipment option |
| 15 | Deficit push-up | `push-up-chest` | A coaching note: hands on blocks or plates for a deeper stretch |

## Low priority

| # | Exercise | Why it isn't needed now |
|---|---|---|
| 16 | Arnold press | A dumbbell shoulder press with a twist. The overhead press already covers dumbbell pressing, and the twist adds little |
| 17 | Seal row / Pendlay row | The seal row is close to the chest-supported row. The Pendlay row is close to the barbell bent-over row |
| 18 | Good morning | A barbell hip hinge very similar to the RDL and stiff-leg deadlift (hamstrings already have 7 options). It is harder to learn and adds little |
| 19 | Spider curl | Same idea as the preacher curl. Biceps already have 9 options |
| 20 | Concentration curl | Same as a dumbbell curl with the arm braced. Biceps already have 9 options |
| 21 | JM press | A cross between the close-grip bench press and the skull crusher, both already in the library. Advanced and niche |
| 22 | Pendulum squat | Close to the hack squat, and the machine is rare in gyms |
| 23 | Kettlebell swing | A power and conditioning exercise. It's a fast, explosive movement rather than a muscle-building one, and the app's targets are about muscle size and shape |

## Suggested next step

Run the measured check on the four **Strong** exercises and the five **Fold into existing** changes. The fold-ins are small data edits with no new records.

Then decide on the **Worth testing** group one at a time using the measured results. Leave the **Low priority** group unless a specific need comes up.

## Appendix: the five turned-down exercises, reconsidered

These were turned down in earlier stages. The reasons are in `PHASE-7-PILOT-DECISION-COVERAGE.md` and `PHASE-7-STAGE-5-DECISION-COVERAGE-REVIEW.md`. Plain-language summary:

| Exercise | What makes it different | Why it was turned down | Could be revisited by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bayesian cable curl** | Arm is behind the body, so the biceps work hardest when fully stretched. The cable keeps tension there, where a dumbbell doesn't | Biceps already had 9 options, including the incline dumbbell curl, which also stretches the biceps | Adding it as the cable "stretched" curl, since no current curl keeps tension at the stretch |
| **Cable Y-raise** | Raises the arms in a "Y" overhead, training the lower and middle traps and shoulder-blade control as well as the delts | For side delts it is not better than a lateral raise. The app has no lower-traps or posture target to recommend it for | A posture or shoulder-health goal for the lower traps (a taxonomy change) |
| **Step-up** | Single-leg, easy on the knees, easy to learn, low soreness. A box height can bias the glutes | Six single-leg squat and lunge options already existed | Giving it a bodyweight + box option, since no lunge or split squat can be done without weights in the app today |
| **Inverted row** | The main bodyweight way to train the upper back (pulling horizontally) | At home it needs a low bar, rings or a suspension strap, which the app has no equipment option for. In a gym it just displaced existing rows | Adding a "rings / suspension trainer" equipment option; back thickness has **no** bodyweight option today |
| **Dead hang** | Hanging from a bar: grip endurance, shoulder stretch and decompression | It doesn't directly train any of the app's muscle targets or functional goals. The app counts grip work as only indirect for the forearm muscles | Adding it as a library exercise without a target, like the farmer's carry, or adding a new "grip strength" goal for it and the farmer's carry |
